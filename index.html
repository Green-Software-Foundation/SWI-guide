Getting Started

Getting Started

This site is draft guidance. It is a Standards Working Group (SSWG) work item within the Green Software Foundation and is not yet officially endorsed. Content here — including page structure, worked examples, and data-source pointers — should be expected to change as the SSWG's work progresses.

What this site is​

This is the SWI Guidance site: a non-normative companion to the Software Water Intensity (SWI) Specification. It follows the same relationship the Green Software Foundation's SCI Guidance project has to the Software Carbon Intensity (SCI) specification: the specification states requirements; the guidance explains how to fulfil them, with explanation, worked examples, and data-source references.

The two documents are kept deliberately separate. Worked examples, diagrams, and data-source guidance need to be revised often — as new datasets appear, as methodology matures — without triggering the version-control obligations that apply to a normative specification. As the SSWG puts it: the specification states the principle; the guidelines explain how to fulfil it.

The SWI formula, at a glance​

SWI is a rate: the water attributable to a software system, expressed per functional unit.

$$SWI = \frac{W}{R}$$

Total water $W$ is the sum of operational water and embodied water:

$$W = W_O + W_M$$

Every component is then quantified on both a consumption basis and a withdrawal basis, and reported both unadjusted (a physical water volume) and stress-adjusted (weighted by local water scarcity):

$$SWIb = \frac{W_b}{R}, \qquad SWI_b^{adj} = \frac{\sum_i \left(W{b,i} \times CF_{b,i}\right)}{R}, \qquad b \in {c, w}$$

If any of that is unfamiliar, start with Formula Walkthrough, which breaks down every term, or jump straight to a worked example.
